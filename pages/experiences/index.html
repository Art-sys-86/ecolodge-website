Experiences

Ways to lose track of time in Panbang!

Forest Melody

Each day breaks with wings stirring above Panbang. More than five hundred kinds of birds live here. Twenty two face extinction worldwide. This place, Zhemgang, slips beneath most eyes despite its weight in feathers and flight. Through foggy light, hornbills slice air while a Ward's Trogon answers from high trees. What unfolds every ordinary Tuesday draws watchers who cross oceans without hesitation.

Bird Watching

The River Gives What It Wants

Fresh from mountain heights, the Mangde Chhu slips through silence icy, pure, almost untouched. A shadow moves below: Golden Mahseer, scarce now, can grow as heavy as a grown child, known for fierce runs when hooked. Instead of keeping it, you let go, because Bhutan says so by rule. For just an instant, your hands know rarity; then it’s gone, leaving echoes that hum softly behind your thoughts all afternoon.

Fly Fishing Catch & Release

Forest Melody

Bhutan's oldest park. 29 wild tigers recorded in 2022. Eight wild cat species sharing one forest. One-horned rhinos, elephants, clouded leopards — all within an hour of Panbang. Most tourists never reach the core zone. Your guide knows where to look. Bring patience. Bring silence. Leave the itinerary behind.

Royal Manas Visit

Two Reasons To Walk Further

Sound arrives first, then the sight of twin falls meeting in a single pool. Returning, there’s Bjoka houses raised by Kheng hands using methods forgotten elsewhere in Bhutan. A single day holds moments that linger long after. These stay with you, spoken again and again without plan.

Twin Waterfall & Bjoka Village

13KM Of No Going Back

Panbang lies where two major rivers in Bhutan meet. Centuries ago, the Mangde Chhu began shaping cliffs that now tower above rushing water pockets of calm come only after tough stretches. When you finally step out below, drenched and different inside, returning to everyday life somehow seems harder than before.

White River Rafting

Go Deep

Most of Zhemgang is trees. Paths through them exist because people needed to get to temples, not for visitors. Moving along these routes beside someone raised nearby turns each step into quiet trust instead of exercise. Spend hours after dark under open stars. Not a single glow from bulbs or screens. All black above except endless sparks. Wild plants growing without asking permission.

Hiking & Camping

Three Hours. That's The Point

Out here in Assam, space spreads wide, creatures amble slow. Around sixty kinds of mammals call this place home. Tall grass sways, elephants pass through. By the riverside, rhinos stand still. Up high, a Golden Langur peers down lives only right here. The person driving decides when to pause, when silence works best. Nothing promised. Reality feels sharper that way.
